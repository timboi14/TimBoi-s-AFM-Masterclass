The exam at a glance

Section A

1 × 50-mark case. Multi-part. ~90 min. Big numbers + discussion + ESG + professional skills.

Section B

2 × 25-mark from a choice. ~45 min each. Hedging, valuation, real options, risk, M&A.

Total

100 marks · 3 hr 15 min (incl. 15 mins reading). Pass mark 50%. Recent pass rates 44–46%.

Marks split

~50% calc · ~30% discussion · ~20% professional + ESG. Plan time accordingly.

Open book?

No. CBE format with on-screen formula sheet. Spreadsheet for Section A — use formulas.

Time per mark

1.8 mins/mark writing time. 18 mins for 10 marks. Move on when meter hits zero.

Section A — minute-by-minute

00:00 — 02:30
Read the SCENARIO TWICE. Skim, then close-read. Highlight numbers, dates, currencies. Do NOT calculate yet.
02:30 — 05:00
Read EVERY REQUIREMENT. Underline command words. Note marks per part. Plan time.
05:00 — 07:30
Sketch a structure on scratch: working numbers W1–W4. Set up Excel headings.
07:30 — 35:00
Tackle the BIG calculation (NPV / APV / valuation). Show every workings line.
35:00 — 60:00
Hedging / sensitivity / second numerical part. Reference back to the case.
60:00 — 75:00
DISCUSSION — apply to scenario, name the company, quote figures.
75:00 — 85:00
ESG (3 marks) + Professional skills (4 skills × 1–2 marks each).
85:00 — 90:00
RECOMMENDATION — clear, scenario-specific. Then proof-read structure.

Professional skills (~20% of marks)

Communication

Structured, signposted answer. Use headings. Lead with conclusion. Match audience.

Analysis & Evaluation

Break problem down. Compare alternatives in numbers AND words. Sensitivities.

Scepticism

Stress-test assumptions. Flag bias. Question the synergy / forecast.

Commercial Acumen

Strategic context. Industry awareness. Practical implementation feasibility.

Mower's deliberate-skill trick: after EVERY long answer, write 1 bullet per skill explicitly tied to the scenario. 4 bullets = ~4 marks for ~3 minutes' work.

ESG marks (Sep/Dec 2025+)

Sep/Dec 2025 examiner: "ESG marks are earned by candidates who identify specific scenario issues, recommend feasible actions, and link those actions to financial or reputational outcomes — not by candidates who reproduce textbook descriptions of what ESG stands for."

1 — Scenario issue

Pick a SPECIFIC ESG fact from the case (carbon, supply chain, governance, gender pay).

2 — Feasible action

Recommend a specific, costed action — not "be more sustainable".

3 — Outcome

"Avoids £2m fine"; "saves 30bp on green-loan refinancing"; "protects sponsor relationship".

3 marks for 3 sentences. Easy if you remember the formula: Issue → Action → Outcome.

Discussion-mark essay framework N · E · A

Every discussion question (~30 marks per paper) follows the same hidden examiner mark scheme: NAME the concept · EXPLAIN how it works · APPLY to the scenario figures. Generic textbook bullets earn the EXPLAIN mark only — typically 1 of 3. To bank the full 3 marks per point, every paragraph must hit all three Ns. Below is a worked example for an examiner-favourite question.

N · NAME

Identify the precise term

Use ACCA terminology: "agency cost" not "manager problem"; "riba" not "interest"; "basis risk" not "hedge mismatch"; "loss aversion" not "fear of losing". Naming a concept correctly = examiner can tick the mark scheme box instantly.

E · EXPLAIN

One sentence of mechanism

Why does it occur? What\'s the cause-and-effect? Use linkers: "because", "this leads to", "resulting in". No padding, no generic textbook intro. One tight sentence per concept.

A · APPLY

Cite a scenario figure

Quote a number, name, or fact from the case. "the £8m capex on emissions abatement"; "Drimpton\'s 30% gearing"; "the synergy estimate of $14.2m claimed by management". No application = no application mark.

Worked example — "Discuss why synergy is often overestimated" (5 marks)

A real ACCA past-paper question (Theory Q19). Below: the BAD answer (1/5) and the GOOD answer (5/5).

BAD — scores 1/5

"Synergy is often overestimated. Companies pay too much for acquisitions. Sometimes deals fail because of integration problems. Managers are sometimes overconfident. Advisers can be biased. Cultural differences cause issues."

Diagnosis: 6 generic bullets, no NAMING (no "agency cost", no "M&A wave"), no EXPLAINING (why?), no APPLYING (no scenario figures). Examiner ticks 1 conceptual mark.

GOOD — scores 5/5

1. M&A waves and cheap credit. [N] M&A activity tends to be driven by cheap credit; [E] at the peak of a wave there is competition for targets, increasing acquisition premiums; [A] given Hav Co is bidding during a period of low interest rates and the deal is the third in 18 months, premium overpayment is likely.

2. Adviser conflict of interest. [N] Investment banks earn a large share of fees from M&A completion; [E] this means their valuation advice is potentially biased toward closing the deal; [A] Hav Co should ensure the bank advising on synergy is separate from the team executing the transaction.

3. Hubris / management overconfidence. [N] Acquirers may overestimate synergy and underestimate the time to deliver it; [E] reluctance to admit mistakes once committed compounds the loss; [A] Hav Co\'s previous track record (CEO note: 4 of 6 past acquisitions destroyed value) signals this risk is live, not theoretical.

Steps to address (1 mark): allocate synergy ownership to a senior manager with measurable targets; insist on independent due diligence; set a board-approved walk-away premium and enforce it.

Mark-banking signpost templates

Drop these phrases into the start of every discussion paragraph. Each templates the N+E+A pattern automatically:

  • "The first concept is [name]. This means [mechanism]. In Drimpton\'s case, [scenario figure]…"
  • "A second factor is [name], which arises because [cause]. The case shows [evidence]…"
  • "A particular concern here is [name] — [why]. With [scenario figure], the company should…"
  • "On the other hand, [counter-name] suggests [explanation]. However, given [scenario fact], this concern is mitigated."
  • "In conclusion, the dominant risk is [named concept] because of [scenario fact], so the recommended action is [specific step]."

Memorise these. Internalise the rhythm. The Theory bank gives you the NAMES — your job in the exam is the APPLICATION.

Common traps — by topic

NPV with inflation

Mixing real CFs with nominal Ke caps marks. Per-line inflation rate. Tax 1-yr lag. WC release at end.

FX hedging

Wrong bid/offer side. Premium not future-valued. Wrong option type. Recommendation forgotten.

IR risk

FRA at wrong side. Annual rate not periodised. QSD direction wrong. Swap diagram missing.

M&A

Confused stand-alone with max bid. Bootstrapping not flagged. No 2-sided cash/share comment.

Real options

Pa/Pe swapped. σ assumed = β. No discussion. Total project value not stated.

APV

WACC used inside APV. Subsidy not netted of (1−T). Tax shield discounted at WACC.

VaR

z-value not quoted. 1-tail vs 2-tail confusion. Limitations superficial.

Valuation

FCFE discounted at WACC. Reinvestment shown as dep alone. Book values used in WACC.

7 days before exam

  • Day −7: Sit a full Section A timed. Mark with model answer. Identify 2 weak topics.
  • Day −6: Drill weakest topic — re-do 3 past papers. Use the Coach.
  • Day −5: Drill 2nd-weakest topic. Sit a Section B 25-marker.
  • Day −4: Formula sheet — recite it cold. Then test yourself.
  • Day −3: ESG + professional-skills phrase bank. Write 3 generic openers per skill.
  • Day −2: Quick-fire 5×90s drills. No new content. Don't burn out.
  • Day −1: Scan formula sheet. Sleep 8 hrs. Confidence over content.
  • EXAM DAY: Eat. Hydrate. Read every requirement TWICE. Time-box rigorously.
