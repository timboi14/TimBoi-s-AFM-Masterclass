What makes a useful follow-up?

If the method is wrong, ask whose cash flow or exposure it is. If the method is right, ask for one numerical checkpoint. If the calculation is right, ask what it means for the company and what could change the recommendation.

After success, change one assumption as a clearly hypothetical variant. End with a delayed retry and a different method family.
