## Question 1

### Exhibit 1 — International investment in Edricer

Drimpton Co is a large listed company based in the country of Comptia (currency dollar ($)). It manufactures air conditioning units for homes and commercial buildings. It is regarded as a market leader as a result of its investment in the continual development of its products.

Drimpton Co has expanded its sales to foreign countries over time. Recently, it has started selling to the country of Edricer and the countries surrounding it, which are located in a different continent to Drimpton Co. Edricer and the surrounding countries have recently established a single market, the Central Trade Area (CTA), with the same regulations affecting how businesses operate. They have also entered monetary union and share a common currency, the peso (P). Comptia is not a member of the CTA.

There has been increased demand for air conditioning in the countries in the CTA, due to higher summer temperatures. However, the number of companies selling air conditioning units in these countries has been increasing. Many companies based in the CTA have focused on selling air conditioning units to homes, and are competing on price. These units are smaller and therefore better suited to homes than the units which Drimpton Co currently manufactures.

Drimpton Co is proposing to establish a subsidiary in Edricer, which would focus on manufacturing smaller air conditioning units for sale to homes in the CTA. Labour costs for the subsidiary in the CTA are forecast to be lower than they would be if the air conditioning units were manufactured in Comptia.

### Exhibit 3 — ESG concerns

The majority of Drimpton Co’s shares are held by institutional investors. There have recently been reports in Comptia’s media about institutional investors’ varying attitudes towards companies fulfilling environmental, social and governance (ESG) criteria. Some institutional investors are still judging companies mainly on financial grounds, focusing on the aspects of ESG which most affect profit, for example, strong financial controls being part of governance. However, an increasing number of investors are going beyond financial considerations and assessing the sustainability of companies’ business models in the context of their record in relation to ESG criteria. These investors want companies to take sufficient actions to fulfil ESG criteria across their whole business and clearly disclose what they have been doing in their annual reports.

Drimpton Co’s board is increasingly concerned about how its business will be judged using ESG criteria. It believes that it has robust governance systems. It feels that Drimpton Co treats its employees well in Comptia and pays them relatively high wages. However, the board is aware that labour costs for the proposed subsidiary in Edricer have been forecast on the basis of paying employees the average wage for their jobs in Edricer. The average wage in Edricer and the rest of the CTA is lower than in Comptia.

Drimpton Co’s board is also concerned about studies which highlight adverse environmental impacts of air conditioning units. One aspect is that their emissions contribute significantly to global warming. Worldwide pressures over the last few years have resulted in governments introducing regulations requiring manufacturers of air conditioning units to restrict the level of emissions. There is also widespread concern about air conditioning units contributing significantly to electricity usage in hotter countries. Countries in the CTA are all considered to be hotter countries. Research is currently being conducted to develop alternative methods of cooling.

### Requirements (50 marks)

(a) Explain the advantages for Drimpton Co of establishing a manufacturing subsidiary within the Central Trade Area (CTA).

(5 marks)

(i) Estimates the net present value of Drimpton Co’s investment in Edricer;

(20 marks)

(6 marks)

## Question 2

### Exhibit 1 — Halstock Co and Marnhall Co

#### Halstock Co

Halstock Co is a listed company operating in the fitness clubs sector in the country of Jennia. Halstock Co operates at the high price, high quality end of the sector. The facilities at its clubs include gyms, fitness classes, spas and swimming pools.

Halstock Co is strongly focused on customer satisfaction. A vital element in Halstock Co’s approach to its customers is teamwork. It emphasises customers working with instructors to develop individually tailored programmes to help customers improve their fitness. Employees also work with other staff in teams to develop new services and upgrade existing services. Job satisfaction is high and staff turnover low.

A recession in Jennia has meant that lower price fitness clubs have gained market share at the expense of high-end clubs. Despite this, Halstock Co has maintained its competitive position. The economic outlook in Jennia is now better and one consequence is an increased willingness of customers to pay for a higher quality experience at fitness clubs. Halstock Co has previously grown organically, but is now expanding by acquisition, rebranding clubs which it acquires as Halstock clubs.

#### Marnhall Co

Marnhall Co is a chain of lower-price fitness clubs which was established by Olympic medal-winning athlete Gerd Marnhall, who is the chief executive and owns 90% of its shares. Marnhall Co’s clubs only offer gym facilities and fitness classes. Gerd Marnhall has taken a close interest in all the clubs and has specified the facilities and approach to fitness classes at all the clubs. Staff at the clubs have little autonomy. Customer satisfaction at Marnhall Co’s clubs has recently begun to fall due to the limited facilities the clubs offer and the lack of variety in the services provided.

Gerd Marnhall has said that Marnhall Co could be sold for an appropriate amount, although he is willing to be involved in the management of the combined company. Halstock Co’s board will discuss bidding for Marnhall Co at its next meeting.

Halstock Co’s non-executive directors want to be sure that Halstock Co receives sufficient value from the acquisition. The non-executive directors are concerned about whether Halstock Co will be paying a fair price for Marnhall Co and whether the expected figures for the combined company will be achieved.

Halstock Co’s chair has queried whether the acquisition will achieve synergies. He believes that differences in organisational culture, the lower quality of staff at Marnhall Co and their reliance on the decision-making of Gerd Marnhall will cause significant difficulties in successfully integrating the two companies. He also believes that the combined company may lose current Marnhall Co customers who are price-sensitive and do not wish to pay an increased membership fee.

### Exhibit 2 — Financial data

The current market value of Halstock Co’s equity is $210m. Halstock Co’s finance director has estimated the value of Marnhall Co to be $145·8m, based on a free cash flow growth rate of 5%.

If the acquisition of Marnhall Co goes ahead, the revenue of the combined company in years 1 to 4 is forecast to be as follows:

| Year | 1 $m | 2 $m | 3 $m | 4 $m |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 230 | 242 | 255 | 269 |

The revenue in year 1 is expected to be the sum of the revenues in the most recent year of Halstock Co and Marnhall Co, uplifted by 5%.

Post-tax operating cash flows in each of years 1 to 4 are expected to be 22% of revenue, similar to the margins which Halstock Co has recently achieved.

The combined company will require additional investment in non-current assets of $7·5m at the end of year 1 and $0·45 for every $1 increase in sales revenue in each of years 2 to 4.

Revenue, post-tax operating cash flows and additional investment in non-current assets in each year after year 4 are expected to be the same as in year 4.

Halstock Co’s finance director has estimated that the acquisition of Marnhall Co will create revenue and cost synergies after tax with a present value of $18·6m, in addition to the expected increase in revenue.

Assume a post-acquisition cost of capital of 11% and a target debt/equity ratio of 15:85.

Gerd Marnhall has indicated that a cash offer of $160m will be acceptable for Marnhall Co’s equity. Halstock Co’s board believes that Halstock Co’s shareholders will expect a gain of at least 15% of the current equity value of their shares from the acquisition.

### Requirements (25 marks)

(a) Calculate the percentage gain in the value of Halstock Co’s shares with and without the expected revenue and cost synergies.

(9 marks)

(b) Discuss the concerns which may be raised by the non-executive directors about the calculations in (a).

(5 marks)

(c) Recommend actions which may overcome the barriers to synergy identified by Halstock Co’s chair.

Passmore Co’s board is currently considering the infrastructure which the company will need in the longer term if it expands in Asia.
