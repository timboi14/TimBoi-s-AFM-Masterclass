Question 1 (50 marks)

Exhibit 1 — Northney Co group

Northney Co is a commercial vehicle manufacturer based in the United States (currency USD). It has continuously enhanced the use of automation and information technology in its operations. The reliability of its vehicles is high, due to the strict quality control procedures which it enforces.

Northney Co’s head office and group functions, such as treasury and internal audit, are based in the United States. However, over time, Northney Co has established a number of subsidiaries in Europe, which carry out production and sales activities:

Ibthorpe Co – UK (currency GBP)

Flexcombe Co – Eurozone (currency EUR)

Pamber Co – Denmark (currency DKK)

Northney Co is looking to expand its presence beyond North America and Europe and its sales outside these continents are increasing.

Treasury function

Northney Co’s group treasury function is involved in setting the group’s overall financial objectives. It provides currency and interest rate risk management and corporate finance services for the group as a whole, and also deals with other treasury activities of the parent company. The group treasury function is currently based at the head office in the United States and overseen by the group finance director. Staff work full time in the group treasury function office.

In addition, each subsidiary has its own treasury function, responsible primarily for liquidity and funding management.

Because more of the Northney Co group’s activity is taking place in Europe, the group finance director has made a proposal to relocate the group treasury function to Europe. A separate treasury function would be established in the United States, responsible for the liquidity and funding management of the parent company.

Treasury activities

In order to increase the directors’ understanding of treasury activities, a report is to be prepared which will include an appendix giving examples of:

Intra-group netting between Northney Co and its subsidiaries

Hedging foreign exchange risk using currency futures

Hedging interest rate risk using a collar

together with an explanation of why futures and collars will have been chosen to hedge the transactions concerned.

Exhibit 5 — New subsidiary

Northney Co is considering setting up its first subsidiary with sales and production facilities outside the United States and Europe. The group finance director has suggested a country in which the subsidiary could be based. This country has low corporate tax rates, which are sustained by an increasing level of government borrowing. It also has limited employment and workplace regulation. It does, however, have regulations relating to the workforce of subsidiaries of foreign parent companies. At least 75% of employees of these companies must be home country citizens.

The country recently decided not to join a regional free trade area which neighbouring countries have established. It is currently in dispute with the regional free trade area over the regulations applied to imports into the trading area from external countries.

One reason for establishing a subsidiary in this country is the demand in the region for robust commercial vehicles of the type which Northney Co manufactures. These are needed to cope with the increasingly changeable weather, including more frequent floods.

Requirements (50 marks)

Prepare a report for the board which:

(i) Discusses the issues affecting the establishment of the group treasury function in Europe and the impact upon treasury activities relating to the European subsidiaries and the American parent company. (6 marks)

(ii) Calculates:

The impact of undertaking intra-group settlement between Northney Co and the three subsidiary companies, using a tabular format.

The intra-group settlement cash flows for the four group companies.

(7 marks)

(iii) Demonstrates how the transaction with the Japanese customer would be hedged, on the basis of the information supplied, and what the result of the hedge would be. (5 marks)

(iv) Demonstrates how the short-term investment would be hedged, on the basis of the information supplied, and what the result of the hedge would be if interest rates rose or fell by 0.5%. (8 marks)

(v) Explains why the treasury function might have decided to use currency futures and an interest rate collar as the hedging methods in (iii) and (iv). (4 marks)

(vi) Assesses the political and operational risks which may affect the new subsidiary currently under consideration. (10 marks)

Professional marks will be awarded for the demonstration of skill in communication, analysis and evaluation, scepticism and commercial acumen in your answer. (10 marks)

Question 2 (25 marks)

Exhibit 1 — Mortexa Co

Mortexa Co operates a nationwide chain of supermarkets in Lannes, a country in southern Europe. Lannes’ supermarket sector is highly competitive despite an increase of merger and acquisition activity over the last decade, as rivals seek to maximise economies of scale. In response to a decline in Mortexa Co’s profitability, the directors are proposing their own acquisition and have identified a regional supermarket chain, Yekkon Co, as a suitable target. Yekkon Co is unlisted.

Exhibit 2 — Acquisition of Yekkon Co

Early discussions between both companies’ boards indicate that Yekkon Co shareholders are expecting a minimum acquisition premium of 20%, relative to the current market value. Yekkon Co’s majority shareholder targets earnings per share (EPS) and it is unlikely it would approve any acquisition which causes a decline in EPS.

Mortexa Co’s finance director is proposing a share-for-share offer. To convince Yekkon Co’s shareholders to effectively invest in the future of the combined company, the proposal is to exchange three Yekkon Co shares for two Mortexa Co shares. However, there has been a rumour that a rival is also interested in acquiring Yekkon Co.

The following information is available:

ItemMortexa CoYekkon Co
Profits after tax$548m$135m
Ordinary share capital $1$800m$450m

Mortexa Co’s share price is currently $8.22. Since both companies operate in the same industry, Mortexa Co’s chief executive officer (CEO) has asked you to assume the same price earnings ratio can be used to value Yekkon Co’s equity.

The acquisition will create annual post-tax cost synergies of $27m, based on the expectation that the combined company will be able to negotiate lower prices with its suppliers whilst also eliminating functional areas which are duplicated across the company.

Mortexa Co’s CEO believes the company’s post-acquisition price earnings ratio will increase by 10% if the acquisition is approved.

Exhibit 3 — Takeover investigation

The Competition Authority in Lannes is a government department responsible for promoting competition and restricting anti-competitive practices. As a general rule, the department investigates any takeover or acquisition which creates a combined company with a market share of at least 25%. Mortexa Co’s post-acquisition market share is predicted to exceed this threshold but, at 26%, only very slightly.

Competition policy in Lannes recognises there is a potential for merger and acquisition (M&A) activity to harm competition and reduce consumer welfare, for example, through higher prices, reduced quality of choice or lower levels of innovation. However, there is also an understanding that M&A activity can benefit consumers by promoting efficiency gains, which may result in lower prices, or through investment and/or job creation.

For example, there is an expectation that Mortexa Co will achieve significant cost synergies if the acquisition is approved. In addition, Mortexa Co’s CEO believes the combined company will be able to invest in a new mobile phone application to help respond to competition from online supermarkets and this will create new jobs. Any Competition Authority investigation will therefore balance the benefits of a takeover against any potential harm to competition.

Requirements (25 marks)

(a)(i) Calculate the pre-acquisition value of equity for both Mortexa Co and Yekkon Co and estimate the additional value that would be created by the proposed acquisition. (5 marks)

(a)(ii) Estimate the impact of the acquisition on the wealth of both companies’ shareholders, including the impact on earnings per share for Yekkon Co’s majority shareholder. (5 marks)

(a)(iii) Advise Mortexa Co’s board whether or not the acquisition is likely to be approved by both companies’ shareholders. (3 marks)

(b) Explain the factors Lannes' Competition Authority is likely to consider if it investigates Mortexa Co’s acquisition of Yekkon Co. (7 marks)

Professional marks will be awarded for the demonstration of skill in analysis and evaluation, scepticism and commercial acumen in your answer. (5 marks)

Question 3 (25 marks)

Exhibit 1 — Zulla Co, initial public offering

Zulla Co is an unlisted company originally founded by a group of friends who are the company’s executive officers. It is involved in the successful production of online games and experiences, and has built an excellent reputation in this area. Zulla Co has recently developed an innovative online gaming platform. Zulla Co’s executive officers are of the opinion that they will need extra funding in order to commercialise the gaming platform successfully and are therefore looking to list Zulla Co through an initial public offering (IPO).

Exhibit 2 — Financial information

The company’s chief executive officer (CEO) is of the opinion that the company’s current equity value can be estimated based on Zulla Co’s expected future dividends. The company’s chief financial officer (CFO) is not convinced by the CEO’s opinion and thinks that Zulla Co’s current equity value should be estimated based on expected free cash flows to firm.

The following financial information can be used to estimate Zulla Co’s current value before it undertakes the IPO.

Zulla Co has not been able to pay a significant level of dividends as it has constantly needed cash to fund its online games and experiences development. The company is expected to pay dividends of only $0.06 per share in each of the next three years. In year four, dividends are anticipated to increase to $0.20 per share, and then grow by 10% every year thereafter.

The most recent financial information shows that Zulla Co’s sales revenue was $603m and its after-tax profit was $32m. In estimating the after-tax profit, tax allowable depreciation of $12m, interest on debt of $4m and tax of $8m have been deducted. It is estimated that Zulla Co made an actual cash investment of $20m in non-current assets and working capital.

It is estimated that over the last five years Zulla Co’s growth rate of sales revenue, profits and cash flows has been largely the same. Five years ago, the sales revenue was $358m.

Zulla Co aims to maintain long-term gearing of 10% debt to 90% equity in market value terms and it has 110 million shares in issue. The company’s current cost of capital is 14% and its cost of equity is 15%. Zulla Co pays corporate tax at a rate of 20%.

After the IPO has taken place and the new gaming platform has been commercialised, Zulla Co’s executive officers think that there will be a subsequent 30% increase in the company’s equity value.

Exhibit 3 — Reverse takeover option

Zulla Co’s chief operations officer (COO) has heard that IPOs can place a significant burden on companies. He has also heard that a company can effectively obtain the same outcome, but with significantly less burden, by undertaking a reverse takeover. This will involve Zulla Co taking control of a non-trading listed company and then the listed company will acquire Zulla Co, whereby Zulla Co will effectively obtain a listing. The COO is wondering if Zulla Co would benefit more from obtaining a listing through a reverse takeover instead of an IPO.

Requirements (25 marks)

(a) Based on the financial information provided, estimate Zulla Co’s equity value, based on dividends and on free cash flows, if it undertakes the initial public offering (IPO) and commercialises its gaming platform successfully. (10 marks)

(b) Comment on the results obtained in part (a) and discuss whether using free cash flows will provide a better estimate of Zulla Co’s equity value, instead of using dividends. (5 marks)

(c) Discuss whether Zulla Co would benefit more from obtaining a listing through a reverse takeover instead of an IPO. (5 marks)

Professional marks will be awarded for the demonstration of skill in analysis and evaluation, scepticism and commercial acumen in your answer. (5 marks)
