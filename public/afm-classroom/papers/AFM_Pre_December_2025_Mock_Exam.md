# AFM Pre-December 2025 Mock Exam

## Question 1

### Exhibit 1 — Thelgard AI Solutions Co

Thelgard AI Solutions Co (TAS) is a leading developer of cutting-edge artificial intelligence (AI) software. It trades in its home currency of dollars ($).

The board of directors are pleased with the company’s success in their domestic market, so TAS has been looking to expand services internationally.

TAS prides itself on having outstanding corporate social responsibility (CSR) values and has previously won awards for its innovative methods to reduce its carbon footprint impact. It has a loyal, diverse and dedicated workforce who regularly rate TAS as an ‘excellent’ place to work.

### Exhibit 2 — Nakos Investment

The TAS board researched several countries that could be lucrative for investment. They paid an external consultancy firm $120,000 to provide in-depth analysis and insight into potential areas for expansion of TAS’s services. The firm identified the nation of Nakos as being ideal for investment, due to its central location, cheap gas and electricity, and lenient employment regulation meaning staff can work long hours.

Until recently, the Nakos government implemented a series of protectionist measures on international companies trading in Nakos, including high tariffs and restrictions on dividends being paid back to home countries, making it challenging for global technology firms to operate within its borders. However, in the upcoming general election in Nakos, it is widely predicted by the media that a new government will be in place within the next three months, who will completely remove tariffs and dividend restrictions in an attempt to promote more international trade.

Seeing this as a prime opportunity, TAS is considering establishing a dedicated AI research and development (R&D) centre in Nakos. This centre would serve both the domestic Nakos market and act as a hub for sales into neighbouring regional countries.

The research firm highlighted the significant potential demand for TAS’s software, provided the tariffs are reduced. The strategic advantage of being an early entrant into Nakos is expected to last for four years, after which this project will end due to the rapid evolution of AI technology necessitating new product development cycles.

### Exhibit 3 — Financial Projections and Investment

#### Required Investments

The total initial outlay for establishing the research and development centre in Nakos is projected to be NK3,500 million. This covers two areas of expenditure:

1) Acquisition and Fit-Out of Premises: An estimated NK1,700 million has been allocated for securing and equipping the office facilities. This figure includes the wages of local Nakos employees who will be instrumental during the initial construction phase.

2) Advanced Computing Equipment: The remaining NK1,800 million is for the purchase of cutting-edge computing hardware.

Tax allowable depreciation is only available on the computing equipment expenditure at an annual rate of 15% on a reducing balance basis. At the close of the four-year project, the computing equipment is anticipated to be sold for NK700 million (inclusive of inflation) upon sale, which will require a balancing adjustment for tax.

The office premises and their fit-out are expected to be sold for NK1,200 million at the end of the four-year period.

An immediate working capital injection of NK250 million is due, with no additional working capital contributions anticipated over the project's four-year duration. This entire working capital sum will be fully recovered by TAS when the project concludes in year four.

#### Revenue and Cost Projections

The AI software will be developed in specialised applications, which will then be licensed to tech enterprises, government agencies, and research institutions across Nakos and its neighbouring nations. All revenue will be denominated in NK. The projected average number of AI applications developed and licensed each year is as follows:

| Year | AI Applications Developed & Licensed Annually |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 9,000 |
| 2 | 14,000 |
| 3 | 28,000 |
| 4 | 22,000 |

The current selling price per AI application is estimated at NK195,000. Associated development costs per application are currently NK65,000. Additionally, all AI applications will require additional data handling from a 3rd party company. The fee payable by TAS for this data handling is 7.5% of the selling price per application. All of these figures are quoted before adjustment for inflation.

While the selling price per application is forecast to increase by 7% annually, the development costs are expected to rise by only 4% each year.

### Exhibit 4 — Taxation and Financial Information

Nakos currently imposes a corporation tax rate of 25% annually. In contrast, TAS's home country has a lower annual corporation tax rate of 17%. Tax liabilities in both jurisdictions are settled within the year they originate. A bilateral tax treaty exists between Nakos and TAS's home country, permitting TAS to offset any overseas tax paid against its domestic tax obligations on foreign earnings.

TAS's finance director has estimated the project's risk-adjusted cost of capital to be 12%, which will be applied to cash flows denominated in dollars.

Nakos is currently experiencing inflation at a rate of 6.5%, while TAS's home country has a lower inflation rate of 2.5%. These inflation rates are anticipated to remain stable for the foreseeable future.

The Nakos Krona is currently trading at NK110 per $1. All net cash flows generated by this project will be repatriated to TAS at the end of each year.

### Exhibit 5 — Naiville Dynamic Tech Co Offer

Naiville Dynamic Tech Co (NDT), a large multinational technology company offering similar services to TAS, recently presented TAS with an offer to purchase the entire project in Nakos for $38 million at the beginning of year three. TAS will need to decide whether to accept this offer at the end of year two.

As this is a new project, and due to the uncertainties surrounding the future of AI, the finance director has estimated that project’s value could fluctuate by a standard deviation of up to 40%. The risk-free rate in Nakos is currently 3%.

### Requirements (50 marks)

(a) Explain the purpose of international trade tariffs and discuss the possible benefits and drawbacks to Nakos of removing these tariffs.

(6 marks)

(b) Prepare a report for the Board of Directors of TAS which addresses the following parts and recommends an appropriate course of action:

(i) An estimate of the value of the project before considering NDT’s offer. Show all relevant calculations;

(16 marks)

(ii) An estimate of the value of the project taking into account NDT’s offer. Show all relevant calculations;

(5 marks)

(iii) A discussion of the assumptions made in parts (i) and (ii) above;

(6 marks)

(iv) The additional business risks which TS should consider before undertaking the project.

(7 marks)

## Question 2

### Exhibit 1 — Transaction Information

It is now 1 December 20X5.

Sheakam Co is a large multinational clothes manufacturer, specialising in women’s fashion. Having recently signed a deal to provide garments to a well-known high street retailer, Sheakam is expecting to receive $35,000,000 on 1 March 20X6 which will be invested until it is needed to purchase further materials on 1 September 20X6.

Reports in the media suggest interest rates are likely to fluctuate significantly in the coming months. As a result, Sheakam’s treasury team has advised management to consider hedging against adverse rate movements using either interest rate futures or options on interest rate futures.

### Exhibit 2 — Hedging Information

The current central bank rate is 3.50%. Sheakam can invest funds at the relevant central bank rate less 30 basis points. Several media reports predict that rates could decrease by as much as 0.7% over the coming months.

Based on advice from the treasury team, Sheakam’s board are considering the following three risk management strategies:

#### Strategy 1: Do no hedge

Sheakam would not hedge the transaction and instead deposit funds at the spot rate on the date the money is received.

#### Strategy 2: Interest Rate Futures

Prices are quoted in basis points at 100 – annual % yield

| Contract | Price |
|---|---:|
| December 20X5 | 96.95 |
| March 20X6 | 96.80 |
| June 20X6 | 96.65 |

#### Strategy 3: Options on futures

The following information and quotes are provided from an appropriate exchange on $ options on futures.

Options on three-month $ futures, $1,000,000 contract size, option premiums are in annual %

| Exercise Price | Calls December | Calls March | Calls June | Puts December | Puts March | Puts June |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

It can be assumed that settlement for the futures and options contracts is at the end of the month and that basis diminishes to zero at contract maturity at a constant rate, based on monthly time intervals. Assume there is no basis risk.

### Exhibit 3 — Director Opinions

Three of Sheakam’s senior directors have differing opinions on how to manage the upcoming investment.

Director A is of the opinion “over several years sometimes interest rates will move in our favour, and sometimes they will work against us - it all cancels out. Hedging takes up too much time and effort to be worthwhile. We should be willing to accept the risk of any changes to interest rates without hedging.”

Director B favours using futures and claimed that “futures are perfect as they will guarantee the interest Sheakam will receive in the future. This means we can focus on the core business and not have to worry about interest rates changing. We also don’t need any cash up front like we would if we used options”.

Director C would prefer to use options, as “looking at the futures prices, it seems the media reports could be wrong - we want to be able to benefit from higher interest if the rates go up. I know options are extremely expensive, but this will be more than repaid if rates do increase”

### Requirements (25 marks)

(a) Based on the hedging strategies Sheakam Co is considering, recommend a hedging strategy for the $35,000,000 investment, if interest rates are expected to decrease by 0.7%.

(14 marks)

(b) Critically evaluate the views of each of the three directors regarding Sheakam’s risk management strategy.

(6 marks)

Professional marks will be awarded for the demonstration of skill in analysis and evaluation, scepticism and commercial acumen in your answer.

(5 marks)

## Question 3

### Exhibit 1 — Company background and potential target

Mackosso Co is a listed company producing health supplements which it sells around the world. It is known as a high-end, premium quality brand and so selling prices tend to be higher than many other companies offering similar products. Most of Mackosso Co’s success has come from selling through retailers such as pharmacies, health shops and supermarkets. Its products are not yet available to buy directly from Mackosso Co online.

Mackosso Co is considering making an offer to acquire Ade Co, an unlisted company known for producing and selling protein powder at affordable prices. Ade Co has been chosen as it would provide quick access to the protein powder market for Mackosso. It also has a strong social media presence which Mackosso Co is lacking, and is highly popular with 18-25 year olds who regularly post photos of themselves with Ade Co-branded products.

Ade Co shareholders have previously suggested they could be willing to sell the business for the right price, with rumours that they would be happy with anything above a 12% gain on their current estimated value.
