# AFM March/June 2025 (24/25 syllabus)

## Question 1

### Exhibit 1 — Kampai Co acquiring Skal Co

Kampai Co is a large, listed company involved in the production of machinery for the logistics industry. Recently, it has been growing successfully by undertaking strategic acquisitions of competitor companies. It has made initial enquiries about acquiring Skal Co, a large, unlisted company. This would be an important strategic acquisition for Kampai Co.

Kampai Co has a good reputation both with investors and also with other stakeholders. The company is well regarded for making a positive impact on the environment and also for looking after its employees. It is doing well financially and its share price has outperformed the market.

Skal Co is involved in the production of vehicles for the logistics industry. It is regarded as being an innovative company, which puts sustainability at the core of business operations. Its most recent venture has been to establish a factory in a less developed part of the country to produce vehicles running on hydrogen power. This is considered to be beneficial for employment prospects and sustainability.

### Exhibit 2 — Skal Co, financial information

Currently, Kampai Co’s 225 million shares are trading at $4 each and its most recent profit after tax was $50m.

Kampai Co’s board of directors (BoD) is proposing that Skal Co is acquired:

1. Either through a cash payment of $8 for each Skal Co share;

2. Or through a share-for-share exchange, where a number of Skal Co shares are exchanged for a number of Kampai Co shares, such that the additional value created from Skal Co’s acquisition is allocated 50% to Skal Co shareholders and 50% to Kampai Co shareholders.

Kampai Co’s BoD has held initial, positive discussions with Skal Co’s BoD and the majority shareholders. Skal Co’s BoD and majority shareholders have indicated that a premium on the current value of Skal Co of at least 25% for the cash offer or 33% for the share-for-share offer would be acceptable. This would be in line with market-based premiums paid for recent acquisitions.

#### Skal Co’s pre-acquisition valuation

Skal Co has 40 million shares in issue and it has $80m of long-term debt on which interest is payable at an annual rate of 6%. Skal Co’s most recent profit before interest and tax was $26·3m, after deducting $16·2m tax allowable depreciation and other non-cash expenses.

Skal Co makes an annual investment of $14·1m in non-current assets and working capital in order to continue its operations. It is estimated that the company’s cash flows will grow annually by 4% for the foreseeable future and its cost of capital is estimated at 11%. Skal Co’s annual corporate tax rate is 20%.

#### Skal Co’s post-acquisition valuation

If the acquisition goes ahead, due to increased efficiency and reduction in duplicated costs, Skal Co’s profit after tax is expected to increase by $4·8m annually, and its price-to-earnings (PE) ratio is expected to be the same as Kampai Co’s current PE ratio. Additionally, there will be an immediate one-off increase in cash flows of $42·3m from the closure of Skal Co’s recently established factory which is producing hydrogen powered vehicles (see details in exhibit 4).

### Exhibit 3 — Financing the acquisition

Kampai Co has minimal spare cash resources and it is very close to breaching its current debt covenants. The BoD thinks that acquiring Skal Co for a cash payment will be challenging.

Kampai Co’s finance director (FD) has suggested that a mixed offer should be considered as well. The FD thinks that offering some cash and some Kampai Co shares to acquire Skal Co would be the better choice for Kampai Co.

### Exhibit 4 — Closure of factory

Skal Co has a recently established factory producing vehicles which use hydrogen power. Kampai Co’s BoD is of the opinion that this factory is not financially viable and does not align with Kampai Co’s strategic direction. It is therefore thinking of shutting the factory down once Skal Co has been acquired, and this closure is expected to generate an immediate one-off increase in net cash flows of $42·3m.

An internal memo about the possible closure of the factory was obtained and published by the popular press. The resultant reaction from the media and the general public has been negative. This negative reaction centres around the job losses in an area which is less developed and that this closure would be a step backwards for sustainability. Newspapers have been particularly vocal in their criticism, suggesting that Kampai Co would be going against its core business values and destroying its reputation.

Kampai Co’s BoD is very concerned about this development. One director suggested that Kampai Co should not close the factory. Another director suggested that Kampai Co should not proceed with the acquisition at all. Overall, Kampai Co’s BoD thinks that these suggestions may not be viable and has asked for advice on what action could be taken to the negative reaction.

### Requirements (50 marks)

Prepare a report for the Board of Directors (BoD) of Kampai Co which includes:

(i) An estimate of Kampai Co’s price-to-earnings ratio and the additional equity value generated from the acquisition;

(8 marks)

(ii) An estimate of the percentage gain in shareholder wealth for Kampai Co’s and Skal Co’s shareholders under the cash offer;

(4 marks)

(iii) An estimate of the number of Kampai Co shares allocated to Skal Co shareholders and the percentage gain in shareholder wealth for Kampai Co’s and Skal Co’s shareholders under the share-for-share offer;

(7 marks)

(iv) A discussion of the acceptability of the cash and share-for-share offers to Kampai Co’s and Skal Co’s shareholders, including the assumptions made in estimating the additional value generated from the acquisition;

(7 marks)

(v) A discussion of the financial issues faced by Kampai Co when acquiring Skal Co by cash, and briefly conclude whether a mixed offer would be the better choice (note: no additional calculations are expected here); and

(7 marks)

(vi) A discussion of how Kampai Co’s BoD might address the negative comments made about the factory closure.

(7 marks)

Professional marks will be awarded for the demonstration of skill in communication, analysis and evaluation, scepticism and commercial acumen in your answer.

## Question 2

### Exhibit 1 — Sohbet Co – hedging a transaction

Sohbet Co is planning to sell one of its office buildings and invest the money from the sale in new warehouse facilities.

Today’s date is 1 April. Sohbet Co expects the sale of the production facilities to be completed and $54m received on 1 August. This receipt will need to be invested until 1 December, the date when the purchase of the warehouse facilities is forecast to be completed.

The central bank base rate is currently 4·5% but has recently been volatile. Predictions suggest that the central bank base rate could fall as low as 3·8% by 1 August. Sohbet Co can currently invest funds at the central bank base rate less 30 basis points.

When the investment was discussed at the last board meeting, the directors indicated that they would like to achieve at least a 4% annual return on the funds invested, but that the hedging method chosen should have a limited range of possible outcomes, to make cash flow forecasting more predictable.

## Question 3

### Exhibit 1 — Grand Country Railways (GCR)

The rail network in the country of Northashia is divided between the maintenance of rail tracks and the operation of trains and stations. A single company, owned by Northashia’s central government, is responsible for the maintenance of the rail tracks. The operation of rail services and stations is divided into a number of franchises. The franchises are operated by shareholder-owned companies, with some companies operating more than one franchise. Franchises are awarded for a specified number of years. The revenue from franchises is mainly derived from ticket sales for rail journeys.

The two biggest companies involved in operating rail services in Northashia are Grand Country Railways Co (GCR) and City and Town Railways Co (CTR). The rail services operated by GCR and CTR each include services into one of Northashia’s biggest cities, which passengers use to get to work, and services covering other areas where there is significant leisure and tourist travel.

The most important distinction in ticket pricing for both companies is that the most expensive fares are charged to passengers going to work in the morning and coming home in the evening, and cheaper fares are charged at other times when services are less busy. Northashia’s government has insisted for the last two years that rail fares should remain unchanged but will allow rail companies to raise prices over the next year.

Over the last few years, there have been an increasing number of complaints by passengers about GCR’s train services. GCR’s trains are older on average and break down more frequently than those operated by other companies. GCR’s percentage of trains cancelled is also higher due to tighter staffing policies, meaning that there are no spare staff available to operate scheduled trains if staff are absent. Passengers have also complained about the lack of staff available to help at the stations operated by GCR and the poor state of facilities at GCR’s stations.

In response, Northashia’s government notified GCR last month that one or more of its franchises may not be renewed when they end, unless GCR invests in up-to-date trains, cancels fewer trains and enhances facilities at the stations which it operates.
