Question 1 — Helios Co (50 marks)

You are working as a financial analyst in the corporate finance team of Helios Co.

The following exhibits provide information relevant to the question:

1. Information about Helios Co 2. Estimated cash flows for the proposed project 3. Veloria Co and Torrin Co 4. Financing the project and risk analysis

This information should be used to answer the question requirements.

Exhibit 1 — Information about Helios Co

Helios Co is a large, listed engineering company headquartered in the European country of Norlandia. The company specialises in the design and manufacture of sustainable technologies for industrial clients worldwide, including components used in renewable energy systems and energy-efficient transport solutions.

Helios Co’s board is currently considering a major diversification project involving the manufacture of carbon-neutral electric delivery vehicles aimed at logistics and urban transport companies. The proposed project represents a new business area for Helios Co and the board recognises that the risk characteristics of the project may differ from those of the company’s existing operations.

The chief financial officer (CFO) has asked the finance team to evaluate the financial viability of the project and prepare a report outlining the results of the analysis. The CFO believes that the project should initially be evaluated assuming that it is financed entirely by equity, to determine the project’s underlying operating value independent of financing effects.

The current risk-free rate is 4.5% and the market risk premium is 7.5%. Helios Co pays corporate tax at 20%, payable one year in arrears. Any tax losses generated by the project can be offset against taxable profits from other activities.

Exhibit 2 — Estimated cash flows for the proposed project

The electric vehicle project is expected to operate for four years. An initial investment of $75m in plant and production equipment will be required immediately. At the end of the project’s life, the project is expected to be sold for $48m at that time.

The plant and equipment are expected to have a realisable value of $22m, which is included within the estimated project disposal value.

Tax-allowable depreciation is available on the plant and machinery at a rate of 18% per year on a reducing balance basis.

The estimated revenues from the project are $12m for the first year, followed by $44m in the second year. The sales revenue is expected to grow at 15% per annum for the next two years.

The operating costs are estimated at 125% and 85% of sales revenue for the first and second year respectively, before settling at 45% for the last two years.

The project requires an initial investment in working capital of $12m immediately. Subsequently, working capital equal to 18% of forecast sales revenue for the following year will be required at the start of Years 2 to 4. Any remaining working capital will be released in full at the end of the project.

Exhibit 4 — Financing the project and risk analysis

Due to the project’s environmental benefits, Helios Co has been offered access to a government-supported green financing programme. Under this programme, the entire project can be financed through a subsidised loan over the project’s life at an interest rate 90 basis points lower than the risk-free rate.

Helios Co’s normal borrowing rate is 6.2%, but the board is considering financing the project (initial investment and initial working capital) entirely through this subsidised loan facility. Financing arrangement costs equal to 3.5% of the gross finance raised will be incurred and must be paid at the start of the project. These costs are not tax deductible.

The treasury department has also suggested assessing the project’s risk using Value-at-Risk (VaR) analysis. Preliminary analysis indicates that the annual net operating cash flows of the project have a standard deviation of $1.1m due to uncertainties relating to demand growth, input prices and competitive pressures in the electric mobility market.

Assuming the project cash flows are normally distributed, the board has requested that the VaR at the 95% confidence level be estimated over the project life (the corresponding z-score is 1.645).

In addition, the board has asked for sensitivity analysis to be carried out to assess how the project’s value may change if:

Operating cash flows (sales revenue less operating costs) differ from forecasts; and

The estimated project cost of capital changes.

Requirements (50 marks)

(a) Discuss the rationale for using the adjusted present value (APV) approach compared to the traditional net present value (NPV) analysis for evaluating Helios Co’s major diversification project, and the assumptions underlying this approach. (8 marks)

(b) Prepare a report to the board which:

(i) Calculates and discusses an estimate of the project-specific discount rate for the carbon-neutral electric delivery vehicle, as well as an estimate of the NPV of the project, assuming that it is entirely financed by equity. (14 marks)

(ii) Calculates and discusses an estimate of the APV of the project considering the subsidised financing and issue costs. (10 marks)

(iii) Provides an analysis of the Value-at-Risk (VaR) over the project life at the 95% confidence level and the sensitivity of the project’s base-case NPV to changes in post-tax operating profits and the cost of capital. (8 marks)

Question 2 — Valdero Co and Fenwick Co (25 marks)

The following exhibits provide information relevant to the question:

1. Valdero Co 2. Proposed acquisition of Fenwick Co 3. Financial data

This information should be used to answer the question requirements.

Exhibit 1 — Valdero Co

Valdero Co is a listed food distribution company operating within the packaged consumer goods sector. Competitive pressures across the sector have intensified considerably in recent years, driven by sustained increases in commodity input costs and ongoing pressure from end customers for stable or lower retail prices.

In response to these structural headwinds, the board of Valdero Co has resolved to pursue a strategy of vertical integration by expanding downstream into the food retailing segment.

Exhibit 2 — Proposed acquisition of Fenwick Co

Following a strategic review, the board of Valdero Co has identified Fenwick Co, an operator of a regional chain of food retail outlets, as a suitable acquisition target.

Preliminary discussions between the two boards have proceeded constructively, and the directors of Fenwick Co have indicated that their shareholders would view the corporate culture and strategic direction of Valdero Co favourably. The boards are therefore open to exploring a formal takeover proposal.

Analysis of comparable transactions in the sector indicates that acquisition premiums paid to target company shareholders have averaged approximately 20% in recent years.

Two alternative structures for the offer are under consideration by the board of Valdero Co:

A cash offer of $3.10 per share in Fenwick Co.

A share exchange arrangement, under which Fenwick Co shareholders would receive three newly issued shares in the enlarged group in exchange for every four shares currently held in Fenwick Co.

Exhibit 3 — Financial data

Valdero Co currently has 120,000,000 shares in issue. Its shares are trading at $4.10 per share, and the company reported post-tax earnings of $34.56m in its most recently completed financial year.

Fenwick Co has 50 million shares in issue and reported post-tax earnings of $9.75m in its most recently completed financial year. As Fenwick Co is an unlisted entity, it should be assumed that an appropriate P/E ratio for valuation purposes is 10% lower than Valdero Co’s current P/E ratio.

Should the acquisition proceed, the board of Valdero Co anticipates that recurring post-tax synergies of $3.45m per annum will be realised. It is further assumed that the P/E ratio of the enlarged combined entity will be equal to Valdero Co’s current pre-acquisition P/E ratio.
