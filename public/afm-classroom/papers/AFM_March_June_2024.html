Question 1

Exhibit 1 — Mahoney Co

Mahoney Co is a listed holding company based in the eurozone. The company and its subsidiaries specialise in large-scale infrastructure projects around the world. Mahoney Co’s board is due to review an investment proposal after being invited to work on a regeneration project by the government of Amasia. The objective of the project is to significantly improve the capacity of Amasia’s largest shipping port.

Amasia has a relatively closed economy, with significant protectionist measures on imported goods and services. Amasia’s agricultural sector is the country’s main source of employment and has benefited to a large extent from this protectionism. The new government has announced a series of reforms, designed to boost economic growth and tackle an unsustainable budget deficit and the country’s high unemployment rate. As part of these reforms, the government is keen to establish a customs union with its neighbours. The principal objection to this proposal is the harm it might cause to the growth of Amasia’s new car manufacturing industry.

Other topics to be discussed at next week’s board meeting include how to hedge the disposal proceeds from the divestment of a Brazilian subsidiary, Oliviera Co, and an intra-group settlement Mahoney Co expects to receive from its Canadian subsidiary, Yves Co. These funds are not expected to be available for six months but will be used to repay a bridging loan, which is due for repayment on the same date.

Question 2

Exhibit 1 — Introduction – Littlebredy Co

Littlebredy Co is a supplier of furniture and equipment. Littlebredy Co is listed on its local stock exchange and is subject to a principles-based corporate governance code.

Littlebredy Co’s board is currently considering two major investment projects. One is an investment in facilities to manufacture office pods. The other is an investment in facilities to manufacture furniture and equipment for schools and universities.

Exhibit 2 — Office pods investment

The investment required for the manufacture of office pods can be assumed to happen very soon and production for a full year will start almost immediately.

Sales revenue and contribution figures over the investment’s four-year life are forecast to be as follows:

Year1 $m2 $m3 $m4 $m
Sales revenue60·086·486·477·8
Contribution24·042·340·134·1

Fixed costs are expected to be $8m in year 1, rising by 5% each year in years 2 to 4.

The initial investment in non-current assets is expected to be $60m. A tax allowance of 100% is available immediately on this investment. The year 4 realisable value of the investment can be assumed to be $5m and this amount will be taxable.

Working capital at the start of each year is expected to be 10% of the expected sales revenues for that year. Any remaining working capital will be released at the end of the project.

Tax is payable at an annual rate of 20% on profits in the same year in which the profits arise. Tax losses must be carried forward and offset against profits from the same investment.

The applicable cost of capital for this investment appraisal is 11%.

Exhibit 3 — Schools and universities investment

Littlebredy Co has not previously supplied furniture and equipment to schools and universities. On the basis that Littlebredy Co is only supplying existing facilities, calculations suggest that the proposed investment would have a negative net present value of $7m. However, the government has just announced a major programme of support for the expansion of schools and universities over the next ten years. If this expansion goes ahead, Littlebredy Co’s finance director has forecast that demand for its products would justify $80m additional expenditure on manufacturing facilities in four years’ time. The present value of the income from this investment is estimated to be $52·7m.

It can be assumed that all costs and revenues include inflation. The applicable cost of capital is 11% and the risk-free rate is 3%. The chief executive has estimated the likely volatility of cash flows to be at a standard deviation of 25%.

Exhibit 4 — Reporting of investment strategy

Littlebredy Co’s board feels that the company has been too dependent on demand from the office sector. Hence it is looking at the investment in the furniture and equipment for schools and universities. It is also considering the production of furniture for other types of organisation. The board believes that decisions it is considering now about investment policy will be important for the future direction of the company. The board does not want to disclose its plans in detail until they are certain, for commercial reasons.

Littlebredy Co fulfils statutory reporting regulations and complies with the requirements of the corporate governance code to produce an annual business review. However, it has faced investor and media criticism that the analysis and commentary on the business which it publishes in its business review is insufficient. Recently, Littlebredy Co’s business review has been primarily a detailed commentary on highlighted figures from the financial statements in the annual report. The main criticisms of it have been that it does not disclose clearly the business’s future investment strategies and discuss its results in the context of achieving its strategic objectives.

Media comment suggests that the lack of clear information about Littlebredy Co’s strategies has been the main reason why its share price has recently fallen.

Requirements (25 marks)

(a)(i) Evaluate the financial acceptability of the investment in the office pods.

(7 marks)

(a)(ii) Evaluate, using the Black-Scholes pricing model, whether Littlebredy Co should proceed with the investment in the furniture and equipment for schools and universities.

(4 marks)

(b) Discuss the extent to which the investment in furniture and equipment for schools and universities would represent diversification for Littlebredy Co.

(5 marks)

(c) Advise Littlebredy Co on what changes to make to its reporting to provide more information about the rationale for, and the factors affecting, its future investment strategies.

(4 marks)

Professional marks will be awarded for the demonstration of skill in analysis and evaluation, scepticism and commercial acumen in your answer.

Question 3

Exhibit 1 — Garnod Co

Garnod Co was established 12 years ago to provide courses for accountancy qualifications. A major reason for Garnod Co’s success has been that it has been quicker than its competitors to develop profitable e-learning course packages. Apart from its founders, Garnod Co’s staff turnover has been quite rapid. Some staff have not stayed long, either because they are ambitious staff who have made a contribution but sought career progression elsewhere or because they have been quickly dismissed.

Garnod Co was listed three years ago on its country’s stock market for smaller companies. It has since developed governance mechanisms in compliance with stock market regulations. Some of its non-executive directors and investors feel that its culture is still too informal for a listed company.

Garnod Co’s executive directors are looking to expand the company into new areas of training, where there is potential for cross-selling courses and further opportunities for development of e-learning packages.

Garnod Co’s board has identified two possible target companies, Pilsur Co and Marhew Co.

Exhibit 2 — Pilsur Co

Pilsur Co’s situation

Pilsur Co has offered classroom courses for legal qualifications and professional development courses in areas of law, tax and regulation for over 30 years. Pilsur Co is also listed on the smaller company stock exchange on which Garnod Co is listed. For a long time, Pilsur Co was a leading company in the area of legal training and its directors and some staff have been with the company for most of its life. The company has a well-defined management structure, with seniority and classroom experience being important attributes required for promotion. However, in recent years, revenue and profit growth has been slower than Garnod Co’s, because of increased competition and Pilsur Co’s failure to develop effective e-learning products.

The current risk-free rate is 3·8% and the market risk premium is 8%.

Garnod Co’s finance director believes that the acquisition could generate annually $9 million of free cash flow synergies. The multiple of free cash flows to equity is 8 for Garnod Co before the acquisition and can be assumed to increase by 5% for the combined company if the acquisition takes place. The finance director believes that Pilsur Co’s shareholders would, at a minimum, accept a premium of 14% on the current price of its shares, although she thinks that the shares may be overvalued at the current share price and that a free cash flow to equity valuation should be used instead. Garnod Co’s board has set a target of 60% of the gains on acquisition accruing to its own shareholders.

Defence strategies

Pilsur Co’s directors are aware that Garnod Co may be interested in acquiring their company. The directors would not welcome a bid from Garnod Co, as they feel that it would result in major changes to how the company operates and threaten their own jobs. Two possible defence strategies have been suggested if Garnod Co makes a bid for the company.

One is seeking an alternative, more welcome, bidder for Pilsur Co. The other is Pilsur Co making a counter-offer to acquire Garnod Co.

Exhibit 3 — Marhew Co

Marhew Co is an unlisted company which is smaller than Garnod Co or Pilsur Co. The company was founded five years ago by husband and wife, Richard and Katy, who own 80% of its share capital between them. The company provides professional development training in areas such as management and personal skills, also training in legal requirements such as health and safety. Richard and Katy have recruited lecturers whom they feel will work well with them, and have given lecturers considerable autonomy in delivering courses and gaining new work. The board consists of Richard and Katy and two other lecturers, but in practice Richard and Katy make all major decisions themselves.

Marhew Co’s revenues and profits have increased in the last two years at a greater % rate than either Garnod Co or Pilsur Co. Marhew Co has developed a limited number of computer-based courses, but has had insufficient funds to develop significant in-house e-learning capabilities.

Richard and Katy have indicated that they are willing to discuss a bid with Garnod Co.

Requirements (25 marks)

(a) Analyse the suitability of Pilsur Co and Marhew Co as possible takeover targets for Garnod Co.

Note: No calculations are required for part (a).

(6 marks)

(b) Calculate:

the minimum price which Pilsur Co’s shareholders are expected to accept;

the target price which Garnod Co would pay, based on a free cash flow to equity valuation, on the basis of 60% of the gains from the acquisition accruing to its shareholders;

the percentage of gains from the acquisition which would accrue to Garnod Co’s shareholders if Garnod Co paid the minimum price which Pilsur Co’s shareholders are expected to accept, based on a free cash flow to equity valuation.

Note: No discussion is required for part (b).
