# Section A

## Question 1 — Pittu Co (50 marks)

The following exhibits provide information relevant to the question:

1. Pittu Co
2. Hedging the project investment
3. Dividend policy
4. BoD meeting

This information should be used to answer the question requirements.

### Exhibit 1 — Pittu Co

Pittu Co is an established, listed company involved in the manufacture of baby products, with one overseas subsidiary company. Pittu Co has a stable shareholder base and its share price has performed in line with its main rival companies in recent years.

Pittu Co’s board of directors (BoD) recently agreed to invest in a major project overseas in the country of Wambatu (currency W£). The BoD thinks that the project will generate a positive net present value and lead to an increase in Pittu Co’s share price, and therefore it should be accepted. However, the BoD is concerned that this may affect the dividends paid by Pittu Co and how this might be viewed by the shareholders. Comments have been made by several directors (Exhibit 4).

Pittu Co normally uses currency forwards to hedge any foreign currency flows to minimise exchange-rate risk, but this time, with respect to the funds required for the project, it is considering using currency futures markets to hedge the risk of currency fluctuations. This is mainly due to increased counterparty risk in the currency forwards market.

### Exhibit 2 — Hedging the project investment

It is expected that the major project’s investment will cost W£400m and this will be paid in three months’ time on 31 August 20X6.

The following currency rates are available to Pittu Co on 31 May 20X6:

| Rate | Per $1 |
|---|---:|
| Spot rate | W£3.5600–W£3.6600 |
| September currency futures | W£3.2200 |

The September currency futures have a contract size of $125,000, are quoted per $1, and require an initial margin of W£10,000 per contract.

Pittu Co hired the services of an economic specialist who advised that, based on sound economic forecasts, the spot and futures exchange rates on 31 August 20X6 can be estimated as follows:

| Estimated rate on 31 August 20X6 | Per $1 |
|---|---:|
| Spot rate | W£3.1200–W£3.2000 |
| September futures rate | W£2.9500 |

The BoD has decided to use these estimates to assess the impact of hedging on the risk of currency fluctuations.

The BoD has heard that using futures to hedge currency flows involves Pittu Co paying margin costs and may incur costs on a daily basis because futures are marked to market. The BoD wants to know how these costs would affect Pittu Co based on the following daily price movements once the futures hedge has commenced:

| Futures price per $1 at end of | Price |
|---|---:|
| Day 1 | W£3.2230 |
| Day 2 | W£3.2219 |

The tick size on each contract is 0.0001. The maintenance margin, resulting in a margin call, is W£9,750 per contract. The margin call will require Pittu Co to top up the margin account to the initial margin amount.

It can be assumed that Pittu Co will keep the funds in the margin account until the period of the hedge ends, and the margin-account funds will then be returned to Pittu Co.

| Item | Value |
|---|---:|
| Project payment | W£400m |
| Payment date | 31 August 20X6 |
| Futures contract size | $125,000 |
| Initial margin | W£10,000 per contract |
| Maintenance margin | W£9,750 per contract |
| Tick size | 0.0001 |

### Exhibit 3 — Dividend policy

Pittu Co has maintained a stable dividend-growth policy, with dividends growing by a fixed annual rate over a number of years. It is expected that the stable dividend growth rate will continue for the foreseeable future.

This year, the BoD thinks there may be a shortfall in Pittu Co’s capacity to pay dividends for the year ending 31 August 20X6 because of the funding required for the major project.

The dividends declared and paid in the previous four years are:

| Year ending 31 August | Number of equity shares (m) | Dividends paid ($m) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 20X2 | 800 | 112.0 |
| 20X3 | 800 | 121.0 |
| 20X4 | 1,000 | 163.3 |
| 20X5 | 1,000 | 176.4 |

The number of equity shares in issue in 20X6 has not changed from the number in 20X5.

For the year ending 31 August 20X6, the following financial information has been provided by Pittu Co’s finance department:

| Item | Value |
|---|---:|
| Expected sales revenue for year to 31 August 20X6 | $1,500m |
| Sales revenue for year to 31 August 20X5 | $1,415m |
| Expected operating profit margin for 20X6 | 25% |
| Expected interest payment | $30m |
| Pittu Co annual tax rate | 30% |

Pittu Co has the following investment requirements in the year to 31 August 20X6, before considering the impact of the Wambatu project:

1. An investment equal to depreciation is required to keep its non-current asset base at the present productive capacity. The depreciation charge for the year to 31 August 20X6 is expected to be $20m.
2. A $0.15 investment in additional non-current assets and a $0.10 investment in additional working capital will be made at the end of the financial year to 31 August 20X6 for every $1 increase in sales revenue between 31 August 20X5 and 31 August 20X6.
3. A further $10m investment will be needed for other small projects.

In addition, Pittu Co’s overseas subsidiary company is due to pay dividends of 50% of its profit after tax. The subsidiary company’s expected profit before income taxes is $55m for the year ending 31 August 20X6.

The subsidiary company pays tax at an annual rate of 20%, and a bilateral tax treaty exists between the countries where the two companies are located. Therefore, tax is payable on profits made by the subsidiary company, but full credit is given for tax already paid.

### Exhibit 4 — BoD meeting

At a meeting of the BoD, the following comments were made.

#### Hedging using futures

The BoD is very concerned about the funds which need to be deposited into a margin account and also the costs related to the mark-to-market process. The BoD has also learnt that hedging using futures contracts results in basis risk.

Given these concerns, some members of the BoD suggested that Pittu Co should either continue using forwards or use over-the-counter options as alternatives to futures to hedge the cost of investment. These directors concede that each derivative product type is subject to unique risks. Nevertheless, they suggest that using forwards or over-the-counter options as alternatives to futures for hedging purposes may reduce risks overall and increase cash flows for Pittu Co.

#### Dividend policy

Pittu Co’s marketing director thinks that any project which results in a positive net present value should be accepted. The marketing director suggested that dividends should be reduced, if necessary, to fund projects with positive net present values. He does not think reducing dividends for this reason would be seen negatively by the shareholders.

Pittu Co’s finance director is of the opinion that it is necessary to undertake the major project. However, she is of the opinion that the stable dividend-growth policy should be maintained as well. She thinks that asking the subsidiary company to pay additional dividends and using other cash reserves will be sufficient to cover any shortfall in dividend capacity.

However, her concern is that cash reserves are needed for other profitable projects and the subsidiary company’s management would not support paying additional dividends. If so, she suggests that Pittu Co should borrow any additional funds needed to invest in the major project and maintain a stable dividend-growth policy.

Pittu Co’s chief executive officer agrees that the major project should be accepted and also that a stable dividend-growth policy needs to be maintained. However, he is concerned that any increase in borrowing will breach the existing debt covenants.

### Requirements (50 marks)

Prepare a report for the Board of Directors (BoD) of Pittu Co which:

**(i)** Evaluates the impact of hedging the payment to be made for the major project using futures contracts. The evaluation should be supported by detailed calculations, including the number of contracts needed, the gain or loss from the futures contracts, and the treatment of the initial margin. **(9 marks)**

*Up to 4 marks are available for the evaluative comments.*

**(ii)** Demonstrates and briefly explains how the mark-to-market process would work and calculates the balance on the margin account for Days 1 and 2 of the hedge. **(5 marks)**

**(iii)** Estimates Pittu Co’s dividend capacity and any shortfall due to the investment to be made in the major project on 31 August 20X6. **(10 marks)**

**(iv)** Discusses whether using forwards or over-the-counter options instead of futures, as suggested by some directors, may reduce risks overall and increase cash flows for Pittu Co. **(8 marks)**

**(v)** Discusses how Pittu Co’s dividend policy should be managed, taking into account the comments made at the BoD meeting. **(8 marks)**

Professional marks will be awarded for the demonstration of skill in communication, analysis and evaluation, scepticism, and commercial acumen in the answer. **(10 marks)**

## Question 2 — Colmcille Co (25 marks)

The following exhibits provide information relevant to the question:

1. Colmcille Co divestment
2. Extract from financial statements
3. Other information

This information should be used to answer the question requirements.

### Exhibit 1 — Colmcille Co divestment

Colmcille Co is a listed mobile telecommunications provider. The directors own around 5% of the company’s shares, whilst external investors, including a number of institutional investors, hold the remaining equity.

Six years ago, the board created a new division—a social media platform—on the basis that this would complement the pre-existing mobile telecommunications business and generate synergistic benefits for the company as a whole.

Although the social media platform is now performing well, there were significant problems establishing the division from the beginning. A trademark dispute with a rival incurred significant legal costs over a number of years, and the board has also found it difficult to resource the division with an appropriately skilled management team.

These issues have distracted the board and, as a result, the mobile phone division is losing market share. In addition, the finance director believes the market may be discounting Colmcille Co’s share on the basis of a conglomerate discount.

The board has decided to divest the social media division through a sell-off and use the proceeds to expand the mobile phone division into neighbouring countries and improve the existing mobile phone network’s performance.

### Exhibit 2 — Extract from financial statements

In anticipation of the sell-off, the social media division’s share of the company’s non-current and current assets will be transferred into a new subsidiary, which would then be divested without current or non-current liabilities.

The board has requested asset-based valuations—both historic cost and fair value—and a free cash flow to equity valuation for the social media division using the following financial data.

#### Extract from Colmcille Co’s recent financial statements, year ending 31 August 20X0

| Item | $m |
|---|---:|
| Non-current assets | 1,068.6 |
| Current assets | 42.5 |
| Share capital | 300.0 |
| Reserves | 112.7 |
| Non-current liabilities: 4.3% loan notes | 675.0 |
| Current liabilities | 23.4 |

#### Divisional shares of profit and assets

| Item | Mobile phone division | Social media division |
|---|---:|---:|
| Share of after-tax profit | 80% | 20% |
| Share of non-current assets | 70% | 30% |
| Share of current assets | 75% | 25% |

#### Notes to the financial statements

- Colmcille Co’s pre-tax cost of debt is 3%, and its loan notes are redeemable at a premium of 2% above nominal value in four years’ time.
- Based on Colmcille Co’s current market value of equity of $1,751.5m, the company’s overall equity beta is estimated to be 1.63.
- The mobile phone division’s asset beta has been estimated at 0.9.
- Colmcille Co’s non-current assets are valued in the financial statements at historic cost, but it is estimated there would be a fair-value uplift of 40%.

### Exhibit 3 — Other information

There is no directly comparable asset beta available to measure the risk associated with the social media division. Colmcille Co’s finance director has therefore suggested using the company’s overall asset beta and the mobile phone division’s estimated asset beta of 0.9 to calculate an asset beta for the social media division.

The mobile phone division’s asset beta is based on an average drawn from a sample of other companies in the industry. It is assumed that the combined company’s asset beta is a weighted average of the divisional asset betas, with the weights based on each division’s share of after-tax profit.

Based on conservative estimates, the finance director expects the social media company to generate free cash flows to equity of $21.5m in the first year after the divestment, growing at an annual rate of 8% in subsequent years.

The current risk-free rate of return is estimated to be 3.0% and the market return is 7.9%. The relevant tax rate is 20%.

#### Valuation data summary

|---|---:|

| Subsequent annual FCFE growth | 8% |
| Risk-free rate | 3.0% |
| Market return | 7.9% |
| Relevant tax rate | 20% |

### Requirements (25 marks)

**(a)** Comment on the distinction between a divestment through a sell-off and a demerger as forms of unbundling Colmcille Co’s social media division. **(4 marks)**

**(b)** Discuss the benefits to the company’s shareholders if Colmcille Co divests its social media division as planned. **(5 marks)**

**(c)(i)** Calculate Colmcille Co’s overall asset beta and use the result to estimate the asset beta for the social media division. **(5 marks)**

**(c)(ii)** Prepare asset-based and free cash flow to equity valuations, as requested by the board, for the disposal of Colmcille Co’s social media division and comment on the strengths and weaknesses of each valuation method in this situation. **(6 marks)**

Professional marks will be awarded for the demonstration of skill in analysis and evaluation, scepticism, and commercial acumen in the answer. **(5 marks)**

## Question 3 — Trimpley Co and Rushock Co (25 marks)

The following exhibits provide information relevant to the question:

1. Acquisition of Trimpley Co
2. Acquisition scenarios

This information should be used to answer the question requirements.

### Exhibit 1 — Acquisition of Trimpley Co

Trimpley Co is an unlisted retailer of sports clothing and equipment, owned by its directors. Over the last two years, its revenues and profits have fallen.

Customer surveys of sports goods retailers have indicated that Trimpley Co is rated higher than its competitors on the service provided by its staff, but lower on range of goods, appearance of shops, and online presence. Its directors are seeking a buyer for the company which will allow them to exit the business.

Trimpley Co has just received an offer of $6.80 per share, totalling $1,020m, for its entire equity share capital from a management buy-in (MBI) team.

This team previously undertook a management buy-out of Pensax Co, a retailer of leisure clothing. The management buy-out was successful, improving Pensax Co’s results, and the MBI team has just sold the company. Finance for the acquisition of Trimpley Co would partly be provided by the same venture capitalist who supported the management buy-out of Pensax Co.

Trimpley Co’s board has rejected the bid from the MBI team and stated that it would consider a minimum offer of $8.16 per share, totalling $1,224m, which was 20% higher than the MBI team’s offer.

Other sports goods retailers are considering bidding for Trimpley Co, including Rushock Co, a listed company. However, Rushock Co’s chief executive believes that the minimum offer price required by Trimpley Co’s board represents an overvaluation of the company and that the bid by the MBI team represented a fair valuation.

#### Offer data summary

| Item | Per share | Total value |
|---|---:|---:|
| MBI team offer | $6.80 | $1,020m |
| Minimum offer required by Trimpley Co’s board | $8.16 | $1,224m |
| Premium over MBI offer | 20% | 20% |

### Exhibit 2 — Acquisition scenarios

Rushock Co’s chief executive has asked the company’s finance director to prepare two scenarios. One is her estimate of the best-case situation for Rushock Co as a result of the acquisition, and the other is the worst case.

The chief executive would like calculations under both scenarios of:

- The maximum price which Rushock Co should pay, assuming that Trimpley Co’s shareholders benefit from all the gains arising from the acquisition.
- The percentage gain or loss for Rushock Co’s shareholders if the minimum offer price required by Trimpley Co’s board is paid.

The finance director has produced the following scenarios:

| Scenario | Change in market capitalisation-to-FCFE ratio | Continuing annual synergies |
|---|---:|---:|
| Best case | Increase of 10% | $25m |
| Worst case | Increase of 5% | $12m |

Other relevant data for the two scenarios are:

| Item | Rushock Co | Trimpley Co |
|---|---:|---:|
| Number of shares in issue | 480 million | 150 million |
| Most recent free cash flow to equity | $600m | $90m |

- Rushock Co’s current market price is $10 per share.
- Rushock Co’s current market capitalisation is eight times its free cash flow to equity.
- The calculations should use $1,020m as a fair valuation of the current value of Trimpley Co.

### Requirements (25 marks)

**(a)** Evaluate the impacts on Trimpley Co’s business if it is disposed of through the management buy-in (MBI). **(6 marks)**

**(b)(i)** Calculate, for both scenarios:

- The maximum price which Rushock Co should offer for Trimpley Co, valuing the combined company using free cash flow to equity; and
- The percentage gain or loss in value per share of Rushock Co if the offer price required by Trimpley Co’s shareholders is paid.

**(10 marks)**

**(b)(ii)** Briefly advise on how Rushock Co’s shareholders would view the acquisition of Trimpley Co based on the results of the calculations in part (b)(i), and discuss the assumptions underlying the calculations. **(4 marks)**

Professional skills marks will be awarded for the demonstration of skill in analysis and evaluation, scepticism, and commercial acumen in the answer. **(5 marks)**
